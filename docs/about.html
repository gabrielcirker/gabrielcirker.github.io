About / Gabriel Cirker

Models, methods,
and the work behind them.

A collection of projects in computational mechanics and biological dynamics.

University of Michigan, Ann Arbor

My work here explores how mathematical models describe physical and biological systems: the energy balance of a rowing stroke, the response of different beam geometries, and the dynamics of immune activation and regulation.

Research & projects

  • Rowing energy transfer: periodic thrust, quadratic drag, moving-mass extensions, and synthetic sensing prototypes.
  • Bridge geometry comparison: equal-mass beam sections, transverse deflection, and ideal elastic buckling.
  • MATH 463 immune-dynamics project: a collaborative project with Kennedy Jamison and Cecilia Pirozzi, combining ordinary differential equations and custom SINDy equation learning.

How to read the work

The project sites pair reports with source code, figures, and numerical checks. The rowing and bridge collections also include historical drafts and unused material, clearly separated from the current reports.

These projects are computational studies. Numerical consistency checks, synthetic tests, and agreement with a simplified model do not establish experimental or clinical validation. Each project explains its own scope and limitations.

The original mechanics manuscripts list St. John’s University (2025); the 2026 revisions separately identify the updated University of Michigan affiliation.
